Milk cells keep time: a lactation clock that works for mothers it has never seen

A model reads one milk cell's genes and predicts how far into lactation its mother is. It holds inside epithelial cells alone, its explanations pass their own checks, and an unsupervised method that never saw the timepoints finds the same gene programmes.

Limits

    Where this goes next

    The clock gives a learned readout of lactocyte state: each cell's position on an early-to-late axis defined by named genes. In a perturbation screen on cultured lactocytes, a compound's effect could be scored as movement along that axis, or as loss of the secretory programme, rather than as change in a hand-picked gene set, which this analysis shows is a weak readout.

    Two tests come first: does the axis learned from milk cells transfer to cells in culture, and do compounds with known effects on milk-producing cells move it in the expected direction?

    Data: Single Cell Portal SCP1671 · Nyquist SK, …, Shalek AK, Berger B, Goods BA. Cellular and transcriptional diversity over the course of human lactation. PNAS 119:e2121720119 (2022).
    One observational cohort. No result here is a causal claim about any gene, and nothing here bears on medication use by anyone.
